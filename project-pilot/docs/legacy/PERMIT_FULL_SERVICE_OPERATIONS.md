# Project Pilot Full-Service Permit Operations — Founding Launch SOP

Sprint 3.6 gives Project Pilot the software workflow to operate a permit case from intake through closeout. It does **not** automatically create legal authority to submit every permit in every jurisdiction. Staff must verify the official filing route before representing that Project Pilot can perform a government-controlled step.

## Customer promise

Use this operational promise:

> Project Pilot handles the permit process from requirements and application preparation through filing coordination, corrections, inspections, and closeout. We only bring you back in when the government or a licensed professional requires your personal login, identity verification, signature, notarization, credential/seal, decision, or payment.

Do not promise guaranteed permit approval or guaranteed processing times.

## Operating sequence

1. **Intake received**
   - Confirm property address and project scope.
   - Confirm the Sprint 3.6 authorization record exists.
   - Assign a coordinator.

2. **Verify the jurisdiction**
   - Identify county, municipality, state/trade authority, zoning authority, and overlapping approvals.
   - Use official government sources.
   - Record the official agency and portal URL.
   - Determine filing mode: coordinator allowed, applicant required, or mixed.

3. **Verify/create the jurisdiction playbook**
   - Project type + jurisdiction must have a verified operating playbook before staff rely on a filing rule.
   - Record official source URLs and verification date.
   - Identify applicant-controlled requirements explicitly.

4. **Prepare the application package**
   - Review Project Pilot answers and saved property information.
   - Build the official document checklist.
   - Prepare application information and scope language.
   - Confirm plans, surveys, contractor information, licensed-trade information, and professional documents when required.

5. **Quality-control review**
   - Check names, addresses, parcel/project information, scope, documents, signatures, fees, and agency routing.
   - Do not submit incomplete or unverified information merely to move a status forward.

6. **Filing step**
   - If Project Pilot is permitted and authorized to file/coordinate: perform the allowed administrative step and record the government reference.
   - If the applicant must act: create one homeowner task explaining exactly what is required and why, then return the case to Project Pilot after completion.

7. **Government payment**
   - Record amount/status.
   - Customer completes applicant-controlled payment unless Project Pilot later implements an authorized payment workflow.

8. **Corrections**
   - Record each correction as a structured correction round.
   - Translate the notice into plain English.
   - Separate Project Pilot work from homeowner/professional work.
   - Prepare and coordinate resubmission.

9. **Approval and inspections**
   - Record approval.
   - Add every required inspection.
   - Track scheduling, preparation, result, failed items, and reinspection.

10. **Closeout**
    - Confirm final inspection/approval requirements.
    - Store the final permit/reference information with the project.
    - Mark the service case closed only after the permit workflow is actually complete.

## Customer experience rule

At all times the homeowner should see one of two states:

- **PROJECT PILOT IS HANDLING THIS**
- **YOUR ACTION IS NEEDED**

Do not give the customer a list of internal operational tasks unless they open the details section.

## First launch scope

Operate a deliberately limited geographic/project scope until the jurisdiction playbooks and staff process are proven. A full-service promise should be enabled only for projects whose filing route has been verified operationally.
